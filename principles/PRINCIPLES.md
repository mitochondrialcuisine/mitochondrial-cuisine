# Commitments & Design Principles

## The Six Commitments

### I · Food is information

Every ingredient, technique, and sequence is treated as a signal to cellular metabolism, not merely as fuel or flavor.

### II · Dose is the discipline

Because the relevant biology is hormetic, the cuisine lives inside dose windows. It seeks enough of a stimulus to provoke adaptation and never enough to injure. “More” is not a strategy; calibration is.

### III · Source determines signal

The molecular content of food is set long before it reaches the pass — by soil, variety, freshness, and how living the ingredient still is. A signal that was never in the ingredient cannot be plated.

### IV · The cycle is the unit

Cellular energy is maintained by two motions — building and clearing. The cuisine composes for both and treats the cycle of stress and recovery as the true unit of design.

### V · The honest line

No dish treats, cures, or prevents disease, and the cuisine says so plainly wherever it speaks of science. Established findings are cited to their authors; original theory is labeled as such; the seam between the two is always visible.

### VI · Cite the debate, never sell the cure

Where the underlying science is genuinely contested, the cuisine represents the debate faithfully — including the mainstream counter-view — and builds nothing on the unresolved claim.

## The Thirteen Design Principles

Written to be executed at the pass, not admired on the page.

### P1 · Cook with living systems

The signal begins in the soil; freshly harvested, living produce carries a fuller complement of the bioactive compounds this cuisine cares about than produce degraded by time and distance.

### P2 · Calibrated fire

Live fire is the central technique and the central hazard: controlled heat generates aromatic compounds and mild hormetic stress that can favor adaptive signaling, while excessive charring generates compounds better minimized. Fire is a dosing instrument.

### P3 · Bitterness and botanical complexity

Many of the polyphenols and phytochemicals implicated in mitochondrial and Nrf2 signaling are bitter, astringent, or aromatic — the very flavors industrial food has bred out. Bitterness is treated as a feature.

### P4 · Fermentation as a second kitchen

Fermentation generates organic acids and bioactive peptides and biosynthesizes compounds the raw ingredient did not carry, including the microbial conversion of polyphenols into their active metabolites and the elevation of spermidine in aged and fermented foods.

### P5 · The meal as temporal structure

When food arrives is a design variable, not only what arrives; a tasting menu is already a temporal structure, and the cuisine composes that sequence with the cycle of stress and recovery in mind.

### P6 · Sensory truth

Flavor is real information the nervous system constructs (the domain of neurogastronomy); the cuisine refuses the false choice between food that is good for the body and food that is good to eat.

### P7 · Build and clear

Compose for both motions of the quality-control cycle: the biogenesis-leaning signals of char and bitterness, and the clearance-supporting compounds (ellagitannins, spermidine) and fasting-like intervals.

### P8 · Cook for the gut that cooks for the cell

Some compounds become signals only after the microbiome transforms them; urolithin A is the clearest case, and a meaningful fraction of people are non-producers. The kitchen sources on three levels: the compound, its dietary precursor, and the microbial capacity to activate it. [andreux-2019]

### P9 · Technique is dose

How a compound is handled determines whether its signal survives to the plate; sulforaphane, absent from intact broccoli, forms only when the vegetable is cut, and its forming enzyme is largely destroyed above roughly 60 °C. Cut, rest, heat, ferment, and hold are all doses.

### P10 · Match the compound to the eater

The same dish is not the same signal to every diner; microbiome metabotype, genetic variation, age, and metabolic state all change what a food communicates. The cuisine personalizes where it honestly can and never claims a universal effect. [perrone-dangelo-2025]

### P11 · Compose for metabolic flexibility

Following Foundation V, the cuisine treats substrate context — the metabolic key of a meal — as a design variable, composing lighter, lower-glycemic sequences and honest fasting-like intervals without prescribing any diet to any diner.

### P12 · Fuel is context

A dish's meaning depends on the metabolic state it lands in; the cuisine composes that context deliberately and states it plainly.

### P13 · Name the frontier at the pass

Where a compound or state carries contested-therapy associations, the discipline names the boundary rather than exploiting the association.

---

_No dish, ingredient, technique, fuel state, or dietary pattern described in this work treats, cures, or prevents any disease. The peer-reviewed science is cited to the researchers who produced it; the culinary theory, the design principles, and the honest line are the founder's._
